Visual direction for the party app, distilled from four team boards

Bombay Deco, refracted

Mumbai holds the second-largest collection of Art Deco buildings in the world, and they were built by Indians who folded chhatris and jharokhas into the style. Two of the four boards were already describing the same city. The other two supply how it should feel today: fractured like a cubist still life, and lit like a 1970s idea of the future.

What the boards have in common

Every board is about a threshold. Deco doorways and sunburst grilles. Jaipur's Lotus Gate, jharokhas, stepwell descents. Cubism cuts a room into planes you can only enter with your eye. The wedge cars are literally doors opening upward. A party app is the same object: something you are either let into or not.

So the identity is built on one idea, the gate, and one geometry, the step. Stepped ziggurats, cubist facets, Pana Meena ka Kund and the wedge profile of a Stratos Zero are all the same shape at different scales. That shape becomes the pattern, the corner treatment, the progress bar and the loading state. Nothing else needs to be invented.

It also answers the brief's tension. "Fun but not completely approachable" is what a good door does. Warm light behind it, but you still need someone to open it. Which is exactly the vouch model.

What each board contributes, and what gets left out

Art Deco

KeepStepped facades, sunburst fans, bronze and verdigris, stained glass, the black lacquer Rolls, the Vogue fashion-plate flatness.
DropGatsby gold-on-black, chandeliers, anything that says "1920s themed night". That's a costume, not a brand.

Cubism & Futurism

KeepOverlapping translucent planes, the muted umber-and-slate palette, the still-life subjects: bottles, glasses, guitars, fruit bowls. Delaunay's circles as the one burst of colour. Depero and Boccioni for motion.
DropImpressionism, which barely appears on the board anyway. Literal reproductions of Picasso. Faceting people's faces.

Subcontinent whimsical

KeepThe gates and cusped arches, stepwells, Jodhpur indigo walls, haldi and ochre plaster, Raghubir Singh's colour, truck-art confidence with lettering, marigold as an accent.
DropWes Anderson pastels, "Incredible India" symmetry, Rajasthani costume shots. Those are approachable. We are not doing approachable.

Indian retro-future

KeepThe wedge profile, chamfered corners instead of rounded, chrome as a highlight material, sunset gradient light, Syd Mead's confidence that tomorrow will be glamorous. Omni-cover typography.
DropCars as subject. There is nothing Indian on this board yet; the Indian part comes from combining it with the other three.

Colour

Six colours, two of them bases. Not a cream-and-terracotta system, and not black with one neon. The bases are materials: Jodhpur night and Jaisalmer stone. Rani pink is rationed to one use per screen, or it stops meaning anything.

Day

Stone base, ink type, brass and verdigris doing the work. Feels like a courtyard at 11am. Used for browsing what's around you and for anything administrative.

Night

Indigo base, stone type, brass for the wordmark and the one action that matters. Feels like standing outside the door. Every event page lives here regardless of the phone's setting.

Type

Two families, clearly different jobs. The display face carries the Deco and the wedge; the text face carries India, because it sets Latin and Devanagari as siblings, so a line can switch language without switching font.

Avoid the obvious Deco faces (Poiret One, Limelight, Broadway); they are the costume the Art Deco board is warning against.

Signature motifs

Three, all derived from the step. Use them structurally: as pattern, as progress, as the one animation. Never as decoration sprinkled around.

The stepwell

Pana Meena ka Kund flattened into a repeat. It is a Jaipur stepwell, a Deco ziggurat and a cubist grid at once. Use at low contrast as surface texture, at full contrast as the loading state, and one row of it as the RSVP progress bar.

The gate

A cusped Jaipur arch with a Deco sunburst over it. Closed for events you can't see into; it opens once when your RSVP lands, and that is the only page-load animation in the product. Hover the tile.

The still life

Party objects, never party people. Bottle, glass, guitar, plate of fruit, an auto-rickshaw seen through a window, cut into overlapping translucent planes. This is the illustration language for empty states, onboarding and the share card.

Chamfered corners everywhere a rounded corner would normally go: cards, buttons, avatars, inputs. It is the single cheapest way to make the whole product feel like this and not like every other app. The two-corner chamfer (top-left and bottom-right) is the wedge; a four-corner chamfer is the ziggurat, reserved for the share card.

Share card

The WhatsApp preview is the real front door, so it gets the most care: night base, four-corner chamfer, stepwell texture at 8% opacity, event name in Clash 600, the neighbourhood and nothing more. It should look like a ticket you can't buy.

Voice

Lowkey, specific, slightly withholding. Sentence case always. Short lines. Hinglish where a Hindi word is the natural one, never as garnish. The product speaks like the friend who knows the host, not like the host and not like a venue.

The identity withholds visually, so the copy doesn't have to try to be cool. It can just be clear.

  • Kabir vouched for you, so you're in.Not "Congratulations! You've been invited!"
  • Exact address once you RSVP.Not "Location will be revealed to confirmed guests."
  • Fourteen going. Six spots.Not "Hurry, only 6 spots left!"
  • Nothing near you yet. Host something.Not "It's a little quiet around here 😔"
  • Chalo.The only place an exclamation of any kind is allowed, and it doesn't get a mark.

Rules that keep it cohesive

Always

  • Event pages on the night base, no matter the device setting.
  • Chamfers, never radii. 14px on cards, 10px on buttons, 6px on avatars.
  • Brass for the one primary action per screen. If there are two, one of them isn't primary.
  • The stepwell as texture at 8 to 14% opacity, never louder unless it's the loader.
  • Devanagari set in Mukta at the same size and weight as the Latin beside it.
  • Two-pixel ink borders on the day base instead of shadows.

Never

  • Photos of people in the brand layer. People appear only as guests' own uploads inside an event.
  • Gradients as decoration. The one gradient is the warm glow behind the gate.
  • Rani pink on more than one element per screen.
  • Pastels, cream, confetti, emoji in system copy, exclamation marks.
  • Rounded pill buttons, soft grey drop shadows, glassmorphism.
  • Deco fonts with names like Broadway. Deco is in the shapes, not the lettering.
